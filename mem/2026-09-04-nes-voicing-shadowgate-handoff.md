## Picking this up

Working tree is clean apart from two things that are not this work:
`saturn/run_with_mednafen.bat` carries the owner's own uncommitted
`MEDNAFEN_ALLOWMULTI` line, and `out.wav` is an untracked leftover. Leave both.

The tune is regenerated by one command with five settings that cannot be guessed
from the result, so **the generated file carries its own command line** -- read
the header of `saturn/src/sound/music_synth_data.c` rather than reconstructing
it. Same for the waveform tables and `tools/assets/genwaves.py`.

To hear a change: `tools/assets/preview.bat IN.mid OUT.wav ...` renders the
offline model in a second and takes the same options. To *verify* one, record the
chip -- the model has been wrong about the percussion twice in this session
alone, and every fault below was found by recording. The probe harness for that
lives in the session scratchpad and is described under Traps; it has to be
rebuilt each session.

## What the reference is, and what it settled

`C:\Users\saggl\Downloads\Shadowgate Music (NES) - Entryway Main Theme.mp3` --
83.8 s, the owner's reference, not in the repo. It **loops at 23.55 s**, measured
by chroma self-similarity, and `tools/assets/music/castle-halls.mid` turns out to
be exactly **two identical statements** of that same section (5760 ticks each,
note-for-note). So "the first ~23-24 s, then loop" is one statement, 192 rows at
a 1/16 grid, and the tune ships as exactly that.

Everything below was measured against that recording. None of it was a synthesis
problem, which is why a year of looking at the synth would not have found any of
it.

1. **A bass octave that is not in the original.** Every bass note in the sequence
   is doubled exactly 12 semitones down. The NES has its lowest tonal voice at
   G3 (196 Hz) and almost nothing under 125 Hz; our render put **60 per cent of
   its energy below 125 Hz**. `--fold-octaves up` keeps the upper member.
2. **The wrong duty.** The original's melody has **odd harmonics only** --
   h3 0.29, h5 0.18, h7 0.13 -- which is the 50% square (0.33 / 0.20 / 0.14), not
   the 25% pulse we used, whose h2 is 0.71 and whose second harmonic the original
   does not have at all. Its bass matches the triangle's 0.11 / 0.04 / 0.02
   exactly, so that half was already right. The chip now measures h3 0.25,
   h5 0.14, h7 0.10 on the same note.
3. **Tempo.** The sequence declares 128.2 BPM; the recording runs at 122.3.
   `--bpm` overrides it.
4. **The reduction re-decided the voicing every row.** `assign` gave the lowest
   sounding pitch to the bass voice and the highest to the lead, which has no
   memory of which part a note belongs to. This sequence's second pulse line is
   **the first delayed by three sixteenths** -- the NES echo trick -- so the two
   cross constantly, and taking the higher swapped the melody with its own echo
   on **45 of the 192 rows**. That was the owner's report: the lead sounded odd
   and switched pitch fast. Voices now follow parts (`plan_parts`), falling back
   to the pitch-order rule wherever the parts are not single lines.
5. **The lead's echo arrived on a different instrument.** Once the parts were on
   their own voices, the answering line was audible for the first time -- and it
   was on the 25% pulse while the lead was a 50% square, so it read as a second
   instrument shadowing the melody rather than a repeat of it. The owner heard it
   as a subtle chorus. The original's answering voice measures **h2 0.14 /
   h3 0.31**, which is the 50% square and not the 25% pulse; the two pulse
   channels are the same hardware and this composer ran both at the same duty.
   `echo_delay` now recognises a part that is another part repeated -- here
   channel 1 is channel 0 three rows later -- and gives it that part's waveform.
   An independent countermelody is not a repeat and keeps a duty of its own.
6. **The percussion had been silent since the day it was given an envelope.**
   The SCSP acts on the **KYONB transition**, and `scsp_key_on` never cleared
   KYONB -- so a slot already keyed on was never re-struck. A pitched note does
   not care, because it is sustaining and a new pitch takes effect where it
   stands; a drum does, because its envelope has decayed to silence before the
   next hit arrives. So exactly one drum hit per session was ever audible. The
   session that added the percussive envelope measured spectral flatness going
   from 0.60-0.72 to 0.03-0.30 and read that as the drill being fixed; what it
   had actually measured was the drums going away. A probe striking the voice
   alone measures **rms 0.00009 before the fix and 0.03812 after** -- a factor of
   400 -- and the shipped tune goes from 19 audible drum onsets in twelve seconds
   to 68, against the NES original's 60.
7. **The percussion was the chip's noise generator, which cannot be shaped.**
   **There is no per-slot low-pass on the SCSP.** I claimed there was, from
   memory, and it is wrong: Yabause's slot write handler is `switch (a & 0x1F)`
   with cases 0x00 to 0x17 and nothing above, the last slot register being 0x16
   (DISDL/DIPAN/EFSDL/EFPAN), and there are no LPOFF/FLV/FAR/Q fields anywhere.
   The chip's only filtering is the shared 128-step DSP. So the percussion is a
   **waveform** now: 4 KB of the 2A03's own 15-bit shift register at two samples
   per bit, and the note it is keyed at picks the clock rate the way the NES's
   sixteen periods do.
8. **The octave fold was applied to a merged row, not to a part.** Mine, and
   introduced during this same session. `fold_octaves` cannot tell a doubled line
   from two parts an octave apart, and this tune's bass pedals on G3 under a
   melody on G4 -- so the fold **deleted the bass** wherever they lined up,
   leaving the melody as the lowest note in the row and therefore on the bass
   voice. The tune moved onto the triangle and back several times a bar. It is
   now applied per MIDI channel, before the merge.
9. **Every drum hit replayed the shift register's start-up transient.** Also
   mine. The register is seeded with 1, a corner of its state space, and its
   opening is lopsided: **the first 623 samples -- exactly one hit's worth, and
   the slice every hit read, because the chip restarts a slot from SA -- averaged
   +39.6 of a possible 100**. Every strike therefore carried the same DC step,
   which is a pitched click rather than a noise burst, and the recorded hit
   measured 54 per cent DC against its own rms. The owner heard it as the hi-hat
   attack being crunchy and pitchy. On the machine the register free-runs from
   reset and is long past that by the time a note plays; running it forward 4096
   steps before recording the table reproduces that and takes the worst local
   mean over any hit's worth from 39.7 to 5.8. Sub-bass under the drums went from
   **3.1 per cent of the mix to 0.1** against the original's 1.2, and the
   whole-band error from **0.124 to 0.045** -- closing 2-8 kHz on its own, which
   the previous note had written off as out of headroom.
10. **The key-off was still not being seen, so most strikes stayed silent.**
   Fault 6 added a key-off before the key-on and was called fixed on the strength
   of one isolated probe. It was half a fix. The chip walks all thirty-two slots
   **once per output sample**, and the two KYONEX pulses are a handful of
   instructions apart, so they land inside the same pass: the key-off is never
   observed and there is still no transition. Measured by striking one voice
   every eight frames and counting what sounded -- **one strike per four seconds
   where thirty were scheduled**, and thirty with a wait between the two pulses.
   `SCSP_KEY_SETTLE` is that wait, about two sample periods, and it runs only for
   percussion because a pitched note needs no transition. The owner heard this as
   the hi-hat playing triple beats sometimes and inconsistently: strikes landed
   only where the timing happened to give the chip enough separation.
11. **The 1/16 grid was dropping the sequence's 32nd-note drum figures.** 24 of
   the statement's 328 drum note-ons sit at tick 14 or 15 of a 30-tick row -- a
   32nd offbeat, some of them written a tick early. The quantiser assigned a note
   to the row it *started inside*, so a hit at tick 14 fell back onto the strike
   already on the beat and vanished. Rounding to the nearest row and converting
   at 1/32 recovers all of them: 176 strikes become 200, with 48 of the gaps a
   32nd note where there had been none. Costs 908 bytes of pattern data.

**The mix was swept five times, and the last two were on the chip.** `[6, 4, 3, 5]`
looked right once the octave and duty were fixed, and it was -- for voices that
were still carrying the wrong parts. With the parts correct the bass pedal became
a real sustained voice and the sweep moved to **`[6, 5, 4, 5]`**: band error 0.086
against 0.287. It held through the echo's change of waveform. The drum level then
moved twice more once there was a drum to level at all, ending at
**`[6, 5, 4, 7]`** -- full output. Recorded off the chip, levels 5 / 6 / 7 give
band errors of **0.219 / 0.190 / 0.124** and high-band crests of **2.1 / 3.5 /
7.1** against the original's 8.7. I had predicted 7 would overshoot 1-2 kHz; it
does not, landing on 7.0 against the original's 6.8, and it does not clip either
-- peak 0.88 of full scale, the same headroom the NES recording itself has.
Re-sweep whenever the reduction or a voice changes; they are not independent, and
predicting instead of measuring has been wrong every time it was tried.

**Ruled out by measurement: giving each drum kind its own voice.** The sequence
labels kick, snare, closed hat, open hat and crash, and the percussion voice is
pitched now, so each could have its own noise rate and level the way an NES
composer would. The original does not. Measured at the row boundary above 4 kHz
over three statements, every kind has the same centroid -- kick+crash 8685 Hz,
snare 8426, hat 8017, snare+hat 8673 -- and the levels do not track the labels
either, hat alone being the *loudest* at 1.30 and kick+crash the quietest at
0.69. The NES has one noise channel and this piece uses it undifferentiated; the
kit names are the sequencer's invention. Mapping them would be inventing a
distinction the machine never made.

## What was verified, and how

Octave-band shares of total energy over the same 23.5 seconds:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NES recording | 1.2 | 9.0 | 27.1 | 20.4 | 19.5 | 6.8 | 6.6 | 6.3 | 3.1 |
| SCSP in Mednafen | 0.1 | 8.2 | 26.4 | 20.3 | 19.6 | 7.6 | 7.2 | 7.7 | 2.9 |

Whole-band error against the original: **0.032**, from 0.124 four fixes ago. Loop
23.64 s against its 23.55. High-band crest 9.8 against its 8.7.

Percussion in the shipped tune, recorded with the tonal voices muted so the
strikes can be counted: **200 of 200 sound**, and the gaps between them are 15 of
three frames, 33 of four -- the 48 32nd-note doubles -- and 88 of seven, 48 of
eight, which are the plain sixteenths. That matches the emitted pattern data
exactly. Both the original and this limp the same way, alternating seven and
eight frames, because a tracker on a 60 Hz tick cannot make a row 7.36 frames
long and neither could the NES's.

Sizes: netbin **222,112 bytes** of the 409,600 ceiling, and pattern data **2,200**
(Grieg was 4,526, and this tune was 1,292 before the 1/32 grid). Sound RAM use is
5 KB of the chip's 512 KB, the rise being the percussion waveform. **Take the
netbin figure from the build log, not from `BuildDrop`** -- after a full
`compile.bat debug` the file is not in that directory at all, because the CD pass
runs second and its clean removes the whole of `BuildDrop` after the netbin pass
has written into it. The previous note claimed `compile.bat` restores both; it
does not restore the netbin.

All seven synth host tests pass, plus 15 Python ones across

both Saturn targets link.

Grieg regression-checked by regenerating it with the pre-session voicing and
diffing all 2,240 cells: **identical**. It has chords in its parts and so keeps
the pitch-order rule, as does the capoeira, which has five parts for three
voices.

## Traps this session, on top of the ones already recorded

- **Mednafen's recording starts with about twelve seconds of Saturn BIOS.**
  Music began at 11.90 s. Analysing from t=0 produced a completely wrong picture
  -- a 29.7 s loop, a band profile with no midrange, 0.62 chroma agreement -- all
  of it the BIOS chime and the silence after it. Find where the audio actually
  starts before measuring anything.
- **The engine is deliberately 23 cents flat** (256 samples at 44100 Hz puts
  OCT 0 / FNS 0 at 172.27 Hz). Measuring a harmonic at its *nominal* frequency
  misses it: searching 1179 Hz for the third harmonic of a G4 that is actually at
  387 Hz reported h3 as 0.05 instead of 0.24 -- which reads as "the chip is
  destroying our harmonics" when it is doing nothing of the kind. Find the note's
  own fundamental first, then step off it.
- **Chroma similarity is octave-invariant and part-blind.** It read 0.892 while
  the fold was deleting the bass and the lead was swapping with its echo, because
  none of that changes which pitch classes sound. It is a good check that the
  right tune is playing and no check at all that the right voice is playing it.
  What found both faults was printing the emitted cells against the source parts,
  row by row.
- **The model and the chip disagree about the percussion, and the chip wins.**
  Sweeping the shift register's clock rate offline picks 9.9 kbit/s; recording six
  rates off the chip in one pass picks **15.6**, because the SCSP interpolates
  between table samples and darkens the result further than the model does. The
  same went for the drum's level: `preview.py` had a percussion voice the
  hardware did not, so every mix swept before this one was scored against a stem
  that was not sounding. Sweep the drum on the chip.
- **A test harness of plain arrays cannot see a write sequence.** The key-off
  before key-on is the whole of faults 6 and 10 and it is invisible to
  `test_scsp.c`, whose stand-in for the register window holds only the last value
  written -- and so is the wait between the two pulses, which is what actually
  made it work. Labelled as not-pinned in that file. Anyone changing the key-on
  sequence has to record it.
- **Count what sounded, not what was scheduled, and mute everything else to do
  it.** Every attempt to count drum strikes in the full mix failed: an onset
  detector whose threshold is a multiple of the flux's own mean gets *less*
  sensitive as strikes are added, a predicted-strike-time probe needs an
  alignment that a fractional row length makes hard to find, and a
  cross-correlation search for that alignment latched onto outliers and returned
  nonsense. What worked in one attempt was regenerating the tune with the three
  tonal channels at volume 0, recording that, and counting peaks -- 200 of 200,
  with a gap histogram matching the pattern data exactly. Do that first next
  time.
- **Fixing one voice makes the next one audible.** Each fault after the third was
  only hearable once the one before it was fixed: the echo could not be heard as
  the wrong instrument while it was still being swapped with the melody, that
  swap could not be heard while the fold was deleting the bass under it, and the
  drums' colour could not be judged while they were not sounding at all. Expect
  the owner to hear a new thing after every fix, and re-sweep the mix each time.
- **`preview.py` had its own copy of the reduction**, so any new converter option
  would have been silently absent from what you hear. It now calls
  `mid2pat.convert`, which is the same function the build emits its C from.
- The probe project the previous note describes is rebuilt at
  `<scratchpad>/scspprobe`: SDK junction, `-Isrc/sound` added to CCFLAGS and
  CXXFLAGS, `-DNETBIN` so `synth_target_init` raises MVOL itself, and `SOURCES`
  listed rather than found (git-bash `find` returns nothing -- see
  [[zaturn-make-from-git-bash-drops-c-sources]]). Editing its `main.cxx` to strike
  one voice alone, or to sweep a parameter over time inside a single recording,
  is the cheapest measurement available here and was worth more than any reading.
  **But it is fragile**: a variant that struck the voice while writing a global
  every frame produced one hit and then silence, and the cause was never found.
  Two variants that differed only in that write behaved completely differently.
  When a probe goes quiet, suspect the probe before the engine -- and prefer a
  compile-time constant and separate recordings over a run-time sweep.
- **A high-pass by double-differencing does not isolate the drum.** Used on the
  full mix it reported the NES percussion decaying to 0.47 over 90 ms and ours
  not decaying at all, which reads as "their hit is long and ours is short". Both
  numbers were the tonal voices' own high-frequency floor. The drum's decay is
  fine; what differed was how much of the high band the drum owned, which is a
  level question, and chasing the decay would have been wasted work.
- **Two of this session's measures could not see what they were asked about.**
  Spectral correlation between successive drum hits saturates: entirely
  different noise slices score 0.850 against identical ones at 1.000, so a
  reading of 0.79 says nothing. Waveform correlation reads ~0 even for hits known
  to be byte-identical, because the row is a fractional number of frames long and
  the onsets never land on the same sampling phase. Before trusting a similarity
  number, check what it gives for the two cases you already know the answer to.

## Open, in the order they matter

1. **STALE -- see [[hihat-crunch-handoff]].** `SCSP_NOISE_TRIM` was never a
   working fine control: an unguarded `#define` in `scsp.h` overrode the `-D`
   every sweep passed, so the sweep that chose 5 compiled one binary five times.
   The knob is now the noise table's amplitude. The rest of this item stood:
   **the band profile is within a point on every band that carries the tune**. What is
   left is 1-8 kHz running about 15 per cent hot in power -- 7.6 / 7.2 / 7.7
   against 6.8 / 6.6 / 6.3 -- which is roughly one more TL step. Worth a single
   probe run if anyone cares; it is well inside what one MP3 of one performance
   can settle.
2. **The start-address rotation is in but its benefit is unproven.** `scsp_key_on`
   moves the percussion's start 293 samples every strike, so no two consecutive
   hits read the same bytes, and `test_scsp.c` pins that four successive strikes
   use four different addresses. Whether it is *audible* was not established:
   the spectral-correlation measure saturates -- two entirely different slices of
   the same table correlate 0.850 where identical ones give 1.000 -- and the
   time-domain measure reads ~0 even for hits that are known to be identical,
   because the row length is a fractional number of frames so the onsets do not
   align to any sampling grid. Kept because it is cheap, correct in principle and
   pinned; not because it was shown to help.
3. **Licensing is unresolved and now shipped.** `castle-halls.mid` is a fan
   sequence of copyrighted 1987 game music. It is in the build because the owner
   asked for it on a working branch; it is not settled for release, and the
   previous note's Grieg was the Public Domain choice. Decide before merge.
4. **Nothing has run on hardware**, and that now matters more than it did: fault
   6 was a hardware behaviour no test could see, and the emulator only revealed
   it because the voice was struck in isolation.
5. `saturn/tests/test_music_pause.c` does not compile: `music_start_menu` gained
   a parameter in 659e630 and the test was never updated. Pre-existing, untouched
   here, and it means that test has not run since.
6. **Attribution owed** on `revenant-capoeira.mid` -- unchanged from the previous
   note -- and now also on the Shadowgate sequence, whose author is unrecorded.
